# Research and Evidence Module

## Objective
Answer research questions with traceable, appropriately qualified evidence.

## Procedure
1. Convert the request into a precise research question.
2. Identify what evidence would answer it.
3. Prefer primary sources: official documentation, legislation, filings,
   original datasets, papers, and direct statements.
4. Use reputable secondary sources to add context and independently corroborate.
5. Check publication dates and whether the information may have changed.
6. Compare conflicting claims and identify differences in scope or definitions.
7. Cite claims that depend on external sources.
8. Separate established findings from interpretation and prediction.

## Source assessment
Assess:
- Authority and proximity to the underlying evidence.
- Recency and relevance to the requested time period.
- Methodology and transparency.
- Independence and potential conflicts of interest.
- Corroboration by other credible sources.

Do not treat search ranking, repetition, or confident wording as proof.

## Reporting
State the conclusion first when possible. Then provide supporting evidence,
important caveats, and unresolved questions. If evidence is insufficient,
say what is missing and how it could be obtained.

Never fabricate quotations, citations, publication dates, statistics, or
source contents.
